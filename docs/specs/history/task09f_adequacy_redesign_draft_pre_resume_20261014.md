# Task 09F — Adequacy rubric redesign, discussion draft

Status: active design discussion, 2026-10-13. **Not an implemented rubric or a
new set of accepted labels.** Pause prompt experiments and synthetic regeneration
until the new contract is agreed. Existing implementations, sealed answers,
expectations and results remain historical evidence under their original versions.
This draft does not retroactively change Decision 006 or the current runtime.

## User direction recorded

- Grounding checks source support. Adequacy includes whether the answer resolves
  the question and whether its own supporting reasoning justifies its conclusion.
- Every substantive answer needs more than an unsupported yes/no and at least
  one supporting point. Some overlap between conclusion and support is acceptable.
- The conclusion field should contain the direct answer. Correct supporting
  material does not substitute for a non-answer conclusion field.
- Explore two binary decisions: conclusion answers the question; supporting points
  validate the conclusion. No invalid points should pass, but neutral content
  needs explicit treatment. These are proposed checks, not yet a final schema.
- Enumerate real question types and failures before defining the final rules;
  derive the combined score only after the checks are understood.
- Regenerate the synthetic development dataset and recheck the judges after
  agreeing on the rules. This is a future phase, not authorization to run now.

The conversational flooding examples are calibration evidence, not replacement
published labels: A was corrected to 2 after source clarification; B and C were
initially 0; D was 0 because its conclusion did not answer. The contradiction
boundary was subsequently questioned and the entire rubric reopened. E/F proposed
labels remain disputed; no human label for G was recorded. Preserve this sequence
rather than treating every interim answer as a final policy decision.

## Proposed requirements to review by question type

| Type | Conclusion should do | Supporting points should do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yes/no | State the scoped answer, including material conditions. | Give at least one fact or inference explaining the answer, not only repeat yes/no. |
| Why / rationale | Give the reason or explanatory relationship, not just repeat the finding named in the question. | Establish why that reason applies; address the apparent counterargument where asked. |
| Implementation / enforcement | Identify how the objective is achieved. | Connect actions, responsible actors, binding instruments or verification steps as relevant to the question; a list of desirable outcomes is insufficient. |
| Accounting / modeling | Explain how the named inputs or scenarios enter the assessment. | Cover each requested input and material methodological condition; do not require every reference detail automatically. |
| Conditional predicted outcome | State the predicted direction/outcome under the stated scenario. | Explain the mechanism and decisive conditions without turning a modeled result into an unconditional guarantee. |
| Measure inventory | Summarize the relevant measures. | Explain their functions and cover the requested groups/objectives. Exact list length and essential versus optional measures need review. |
| Qualified feasibility | Address whether the proposal meets the stated objectives, within what is established. | Distinguish policy compatibility from studied engineering feasibility; identify unresolved alternatives honestly. |

Cross-cutting requirements need explicit treatment: multiple named entities,
multiple requested outcomes, negative premises, time horizons, scenario scope,
material conditions, quantity versus direction, valid alternative reasoning, and
qualified uncertainty. C006 asks both binding obligation and demonstrated
compliance; C014/C017 each name two inputs; C036 covers wildlife and people;
C007 combines lane reduction, regional function and two proposed control methods.
An answer must not receive full credit just for answering the easiest component.

C007 is especially important: the approved development reference distinguishes
policy direction from unresolved movable-median/overhead-signal feasibility.
A direct answer can be qualified; requiring the conclusion to answer must not
force an unsupported categorical yes/no. Reference details are evidence for
review, not automatically a mandatory checklist.

## Proposed checks, not final scoring rules

**C — conclusion adequacy:** Does the conclusion directly and correctly resolve
all essential parts of the question at the warranted level of certainty?
Checking responsiveness alone would let a confidently wrong answer pass. Decide
how much detail belongs in the conclusion versus its supporting points; the
conclusion should not have to duplicate the entire answer.

**S — supporting-reasoning adequacy:** Does the answer contain at least one
substantive supporting point, and do its points and material qualifications
jointly provide sufficient, valid support for the conclusion, without a
material contradiction or invalid inferential step?

S combines distinct diagnostics. A list of individually acceptable points is not
necessarily sufficient jointly. Retain the diagnostics even if the public
summary ultimately uses two binaries:

| Point role | Meaning | Proposed treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contributes support | Helps establish the answer or a necessary intermediate step. | Counts toward support, but need not prove the whole conclusion alone. |
| Material qualification | Limits a claim appropriately by scenario, time, population or uncertainty. | Evaluate with the claim it constrains; not an automatic contradiction. |
| Neutral | Neither helps establish nor undermines this answer. | No adequacy penalty by itself; does not satisfy the support requirement. |
| Undermines / contradicts | Opposes a material conclusion or another necessary premise under the same conditions. | Fails the proposed no-invalid-reasoning condition. |
| Invalid inference | Claims a connection that does not follow, including circularity or an unjustified leap. | Fails the proposed support condition. |

These are roles/diagnostics, not necessarily one exclusive enum. A point can mix
helpful and harmful assertions; a qualification can contribute support. A source
claim may be false yet logically imply the right conclusion, so source support
and argumentative contribution are not interchangeable labels. A condition in
qualifications/optional_context cannot be ignored solely because of its field.

“No construction before 2027” is neutral only relative to the particular
question and argument. It could be decisive in a timing or construction-impact
question. Merely placing an assertion in optional_context must not immunize it
if it undermines the answer.

**Unresolved meaning of invalid:** Does this mean any source-unsupported point,
or a point that damages answer correctness/reasoning? Requiring every point to
be source-supported would duplicate part of grounding and is broader than the
proposed division. This needs an explicit decision, especially for E/F: an
incorrect residual depth can still imply that flooding remains. Do not decide
this silently through prompt wording.

**Unresolved repetition boundary:** “No, flooding remains” plus “1.2 feet remains”
adds a warrant even if both fields overlap. “No, it is not eliminated” plus
“because it is not eliminated” merely restates the conclusion. Review whether
that pure restatement satisfies the minimum-support requirement; the recommended
choice is no, while permitting overlap that adds a fact, mechanism or warrant.

## Failure coverage to work through

The proposed outcomes below are discussion targets, not gold labels.

| Failure / control pattern | What must detect it | Remaining issue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wrong conclusion with accurate supporting facts | C; S also detects opposition if applicable | Does correctness depend on reference plus permitted alternative evidence? |
| Generic, empty or wrong-question conclusion | C | Explicit conclusion-field requirement is user direction. |
| Conclusion answers only one of several required parts | C and/or joint coverage under S | Split summary completeness from supporting-detail completeness. |
| Correct conclusion, no supporting points | S | Structural presence alone can be checked mechanically. |
| Correct conclusion, only neutral background | S joint sufficiency | Neutral points neither penalize nor provide a warrant. |
| Correct conclusion, circular restatement | S | Define acceptable overlap versus no explanatory content. |
| Correct conclusion, explicit opposite supporting point | S | Aggregated score remains open; record contradiction distinctly. |
| Correct conclusion, wrong causal explanation | S | A why-question may also fail C because the reason is the answer. |
| Individually plausible points with an invalid connecting inference | S joint reasoning | Per-point checks alone miss the gap. |
| Supported points omit a necessary premise or requested component | S joint coverage | Do not convert every reference detail into a requirement. |
| Correct answer with incidental neutral factual error | Grounding; adequacy effect unresolved | Resolve source falsity versus reasoning invalidity. |
| Missing condition / guarantee substituted for modeled outcome | C and/or S depending on effect | Distinguish changed answer from incidental overstatement. |
| Apparent contradiction across different scenarios or times | Correct C/S judgment should preserve it | Same-scope comparison before declaring contradiction. |
| Concise but sufficient reasoning / valid alternative argument | Correct C/S judgment should preserve it | Reference wording/order is not mandatory. |
| Contradiction or material error hidden in optional context | S or C according to effect | Evaluate content, not only field labels. |
| Many correct points plus one material invalid step | S | No point-count averaging that dilutes the invalid step. |
| Duplicated supporting points | Joint S | Repetition cannot create additional substantive support. |
| Abstention / insufficient-evidence answer | Separate answerability/status handling | Substantive-answer support requirement versus honest abstention must be resolved. |
| Malformed output / invalid point IDs / placeholder diagnostics | Mechanical validation plus diagnostic review | Not equivalent to a semantic false judgment. |
| Adequate answer with wrong citations | Grounding/citation evaluation | Adequacy must not quietly repair grounding results. |

## Aggregation remains a separate decision

Keep the two decisions visible even after selecting a combined score. Do not
simply add the binaries: C=false/S=true must not accidentally earn the same
partial credit as a correct conclusion with deficient support unless that is an
explicit policy choice. Also decide whether S assesses internal implication
independently (a coherent argument for a false conclusion is possible) or includes
premise validity against evidence.

Two options to compare with examples:

- **Single pass/fail:** pass only when C and S pass; retain diagnostics for why.
- **Gated ordinal score:** C=false gives 0; C=true/S=false gives 1;
  C=true/S=true gives 2. This would score a correct conclusion with directly
  contradictory support as 1 unless a separately defined contradiction veto is
  added. That is an unresolved choice, not an implementation detail.

A contradiction veto preserves the earlier B=0 preference but introduces a third
condition into aggregation. Decide that openly instead of concealing it inside
one vaguely worded binary. Neither option is selected by this draft.

## Development and regeneration sequence

1. Review the question inventory and minimal substantive-answer requirements.
2. Review paired examples for each failure family, including successful concise,
   alternative, qualified and neutral-context controls. Decide C/S/point roles
   before looking at an aggregate 0/1/2 score or historical model outputs.
3. Freeze the rubric, aggregation, abstention treatment and diagnostic schema in
   a versioned contract. Reconcile Decision 006, sprint formulas, target answer
   instructions and judge docs explicitly; do not silently redefine existing runs.
4. Write a bounded synthetic regeneration contract. Re-author development answers
   and component expectations against the new rules, with negative and positive
   pairs and source-backed rationales. Preserve sources, frozen questions/splits,
   approved reference versions, existing artifacts and record-level lineage.
   Any reference-answer change must be explicit, not hidden in regeneration.
5. Validate component labels and derived score consistency offline; review a small
   calibration batch before generating the full development set. Define spending,
   execution and retry caps before any model-backed generation.
6. Implement and recheck the judge under the agreed rules, then compare components,
   aggregate results, control regressions, output validity and repeat consistency.
   Grounding artifacts remain versioned; regeneration does not overwrite them.

Reserved challenge and test payloads remain untouched. User review of development
examples is not permission to inspect all held-out question wording. Dataset
regeneration and provider runs have not begun. No commit or push.

## Research and learning

[RAGChecker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08067) decomposes evaluation into claim-level
checks and diagnostic metrics; [FActScore](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251)
assesses factual support at an atomic-fact level. These support the value of
inspectable components but do not validate our proposed question-specific
reasoning rubric, binary aggregation or error veto. Joint argumentative sufficiency
is a separate design requirement. No new framework/dependency is needed to draft
or calibrate this contract. Decomposition improves inspectability; it does not
remove ambiguity about what counts as essential or valid.
